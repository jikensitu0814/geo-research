# Search Central Live Deep Dive Europe 2026 — Talk Proposal Draft

## What would be your topic(s)?

**Stop Chasing a Single AI Answer: A Reproducible Workflow for Turning AI Visibility Data into Content Priorities**

Suggested categories: Using tools and data; Using AI; Optimizing your work.

## What would be the goal and outline of your talk(s)? Tell us more about your talk(s).

The goal of this talk is to show SEO and content teams how to move from one-off AI-answer screenshots to a repeatable, evidence-based process for prioritizing content and external evidence.

I will present an anonymized, aggregated case study of 3,237 AI responses across 10 industries and two generative answer systems: ChatGPT and Gemini. The workflow groups queries by recommendation, comparison, and informational intent, then measures entity mentions, citation-source categories, and day-to-day stability. While the exact patterns are model-, language-, and time-specific, the workflow is designed to be repeated as search experiences evolve.

A key finding is that professional services, consumer products, and technical products can show very different citation patterns even when they face similar visibility questions. This means that “make the website easier to cite” is not a universal strategy. Depending on the intent and the repeatable signals in the data, teams may need to prioritize official explanatory pages, comparison and buying-guide content, FAQs, or verifiable third-party reviews, media coverage, and platform information.

The case study is grounded in Traditional Chinese queries, so I will treat the observed citation patterns as market-specific rather than universal. The transferable lesson for multilingual, multi-market teams is the versioned measurement workflow: test local hypotheses, document the scope, and re-validate decisions over time.

Finally, I will show how to turn isolated AI answers into a versioned, testable content-prioritization workflow. The point is not to reverse-engineer AI systems or predict every user query, but to document hypotheses, identify stable patterns, and re-test them as models and user behavior evolve. Attendees will leave with a lightweight framework they can adapt to their own industry.
